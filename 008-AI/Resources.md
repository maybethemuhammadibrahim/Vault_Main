## Roadmap

### **1. The "Perfect Match" Resource**

**Course:** **CS188: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence (UC Berkeley)**

* **Why it's the best:** This course is legendary in the AI field. Its syllabus matches yours almost 1:1. It uses the same textbook and covers Agents, Search, CSPs, Reinforcement Learning, and Bayes Nets in exactly the depth you need.
* **Key Feature:** The "Pac-Man Projects." This course uses the game Pac-Man to teach AI agents. You program Pac-Man to search paths, eat dots (CSPs), and avoid ghosts (Adversarial Search/RL).
* **Where to find it:**
* **Course Website:** Search for "UC Berkeley CS188 Inst" to find the semester archives. The [Fall 2018 website](https://inst.eecs.berkeley.edu/~cs188/fa18/) is particularly complete with slides and notes.
* **Video Lectures:** Search **"CS188 AI Berkeley" on YouTube**. The lectures by **Dan Klein** or **Pieter Abbeel** are widely considered the best explanations of these topics available online.

### **2. The "Author's" Course**

**Course:** **Intro to Artificial Intelligence (Udacity CS271)**

* **Instructors:** **Peter Norvig** (The author of your textbook) and Sebastian Thrun.
* **Why it's the best:** Who better to learn from than the man who wrote the book? This course covers your exact topics (Search, Logic, Probability, Planning) with short, digestible videos and quizzes.
* **Best for:** Weeks 1–8 (Search, Logic, Probability).

### **3. Topic-by-Topic Breakdown (Mapped to Your Weeks)**

Here is how you can use these resources to master specific weeks of your outline:

#### **Module 1: Search & Logic (Weeks 1–7)**

* **Topics:** Agents, BFS/DFS, A* Search, Heuristics, CSPs, Minimax, Alpha-Beta Pruning.
* **Best Resource:** **MIT 6.034 Artificial Intelligence (Patrick Winston)**
* *Why:* The late Patrick Winston was a master storyteller. His explanations of **Constraint Satisfaction (Weeks 4-5)** and **Alpha-Beta Pruning (Week 7)** are clearer than any other resource. He explains the "why" not just the "how."
* *Watch:* MIT OpenCourseWare on YouTube, specifically the lectures on Search, Constraints, and Games.



#### **Module 2: Uncertainty & Bayes (Weeks 8–9)**

* **Topics:** Probability, Bayes Rule, Bayesian Networks, HMMs.
* **Best Resource:** **CS188 (Berkeley) - Probability & Bayes Modules**
* *Why:* This is often the hardest part of the syllabus for students. CS188 breaks down "Inference" and "Sampling" visually.
* *Watch:* Lectures on "Probability," "Bayes Nets," and "Hidden Markov Models" from the CS188 playlist.



#### **Module 3: Machine Learning (Weeks 9–10)**

* **Topics:** Linear/Logistic Regression, Decision Trees, Neural Networks, K-Means.
* **Best Resource:** **Andrew Ng’s Machine Learning (Coursera/Stanford)**
* *Why:* For Weeks 9 and 10, your outline shifts to standard ML. Andrew Ng's original course is the industry benchmark for these specific algorithms (Regression, Neural Nets, K-Means).
* *Alternative:* **Harvard CS50 Introduction to AI with Python** (Lecture "Learning") if you want a code-first approach rather than math-heavy.



#### **Module 4: Reinforcement Learning (Weeks 12–13)**

* **Topics:** Q-Learning, Markov Decision Processes (MDP), Rewards, Policy.
* **Best Resource:** **Stanford CS234 or David Silver’s RL Course**
* *Note:* Your syllabus covers "introductory" RL.
* *Recommendation:* Stick to **CS188's Reinforcement Learning lectures** (usually 2-3 videos). They bridge the gap between MDPs and Q-Learning perfectly for an undergrad level. If you need more depth, **David Silver (DeepMind)** has a famous lecture series on YouTube, but it is very advanced.
