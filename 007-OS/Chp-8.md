Here is an intuitive way to think about each of the four necessary conditions for a deadlock:

*   **Mutual Exclusion (The "No Sharing" Rule):** This condition means that **some resources simply cannot be shared by multiple threads at the same time**. Just like only one car can drive over a single-lane bridge at a time, certain resources (like a mutex lock) can only be accessed by one process at a time. If another thread wants that resource, it is forced to wait until the current owner finishes and lets it go.
*   **Hold and Wait (The "Greedy but Patient" Rule):** This happens when **a process already has its hands on at least one resource, but refuses to let it go while waiting to get more**. You can imagine a person holding onto a tool they aren't using yet, while standing in line waiting for someone else to hand them the exact tool they need next. 
*   **No Preemption (The "No Taking by Force" Rule):** This means that **resources cannot be forcibly snatched away from a process**. The operating system cannot intervene and take a resource back; it can only be released voluntarily by the thread holding it, typically after that thread has completely finished its designated task.
*   **Circular Wait (The "Catch-22" Loop):** This occurs when there is a closed chain of waiting processes. Imagine a circle of threads where **each thread is waiting for the thread next to it to release a resource, but the last thread in the circle is waiting for the first thread**. Because everyone needs an item held by the next person in the chain, nobody can ever take the first step to finish their task.
