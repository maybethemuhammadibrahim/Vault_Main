## The Era of General Zia-ul-Haq (1977–1988)

* General Zia transitioned Pakistan from a parliamentary democracy to a military-dominated, authoritarian system.
* He suspended the 1973 Constitution upon taking power and heavily amended it to fit his ideology.
* The system changed into a quasi-presidential (hybrid) government.

## Suspension of Constitution & Legal Justifications

* **Operation Fair Play (1977):** Zia launched a coup on July 5, 1977, declared Martial Law, and became the Chief Martial Law Administrator (CMLA).
* **Doctrine of Necessity (1977):** The Supreme Court validated his martial law rule to supposedly ensure free and fair elections.
* **Provisional Constitutional Order (PCO 1981):** This order effectively replaced the 1973 Constitution and stopped superior courts from challenging military court decisions.
* **Oath of Allegiance:** Judges were forced to swear a new oath to the military regime. Nineteen justices refused and were removed, severely weakening judicial independence.

## Electoral Changes & Consolidation of Power

* **1984 Referendum:** Zia held a highly controversial referendum on his "Islamization" program. He interpreted a "yes" vote as a mandate to serve as President for five years.
* **Non-Party Elections (1985):** He held elections without political parties to intentionally weaken the opposition, especially the PPP.

## The Eighth Constitutional Amendment (1985)

* This was the most significant and fundamental alteration to the 1973 Constitution.
* **Article 58(2)(b):** Gave the President the absolute power to unilaterally dissolve the National Assembly and dismiss the Prime Minister (which he used against the Junejo government in 1988).
* **National Security Council (NSC):** Created to give the military a direct, formal role in national security decisions.
* **Article 270-A:** Validated all acts, laws, and orders passed by the military regime between 1977 and 1985, completely protecting them from judicial review.

## Islamization of Laws

* Zia established the Federal Shariat Court.
* He introduced the Hudood Ordinance and Qanoon-e-Shahadat (Law of Evidence).
* These changes created a more theocratic and patriarchal legal framework.
* In certain cases under these new laws, the legal weight of a woman's testimony was reduced.

## Consequences for the State Structure

* **Executive Presidency:** The Prime Minister became entirely subservient to the military-backed President.
* **Unitary Power:** Power was heavily centralized in the President's hands, destroying the federal, parliamentary structure.
* **Weakened Institutions:** The independence of the judiciary was destroyed, and political parties were banned or heavily restricted.
* **Long-Term Impact:** Zia's constitutional modifications, especially the Eighth Amendment, survived long after his death in 1988 and permanently impacted Pakistan's democratic evolution.
