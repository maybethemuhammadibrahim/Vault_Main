### **4. Conceptual: Why does Orthogonality Change?**

You might find that two vectors are orthogonal in Euclidean Space but **not** orthogonal in a Weighted Space.

**The Logic: The "Stretched Grid"**

* **Euclidean Space:** The grid is a perfect square. If vectors cancel out (one goes up, one goes down), the result is 0.
* **Weighted Space:** Imagine stretching the grid horizontally. If the x-axis is weighted by 3, horizontal movement becomes "stronger." The vectors might still go up and down, but the massive horizontal overlap now dominates. They no longer cancel out.

> **Visual Analogy:** Draw a perfect "+" sign on a deflated balloon. If you stretch the balloon sideways (weighted inner product), the lines are no longer . They become skewed. The angle changes because the space itself has been warped.
